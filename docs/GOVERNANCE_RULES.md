# Governance rules

Board definitions are EDU, HLT, SEC, INF, TRD, YTH, CUL. Only authorized, jurisdiction-specific instances can be established. Duplicate non-archived instances are rejected. Establishment: draft → submitted → under review → independently approved → active → suspended/archived.

An adopted charter records version, authority, capacity, tenure, quorum and voting percentages. Synthetic fixture rules are examples only. Active boards must be suspended before replacing a charter. Meetings retain the effective charter snapshot.

Appointment: nominate → candidate accepts → residency/eligibility screening → independent approval → effective-dated appointment and role grant. Expiry/revocation stops privileges on the next request.

Case status transitions are explicit. Ordinary cases require matching board/qualified assignee before investigation. Conduct complaints route to ethics; security cases never fall through to ordinary board access. Proposed outcomes and independent verification are separate, and residents can appeal closure.

Formal voting uses eligible active appointments at the meeting time, verified attendance, motion/meeting recusals, the charter quorum, one vote per member per round, and a threshold computed from present eligible members. Every present member must vote or abstain before the result is finalized. Missing charter or quorum blocks formal decisions. Current implementation supports one electronic round; formal revotes, in-person aggregation, unanimous-consent procedures and charter-specific tie rules remain future work.

Minutes are versioned before approval and locked afterward. An independent reviewer approves them. An adopted motion plus approved minutes is necessary for a resolution. Corrections to approved minutes and resolution amendments are deliberately blocked until a dedicated amendment workflow is implemented. Resolution tasks are restricted to active board members; no government task acceptance is fabricated.
