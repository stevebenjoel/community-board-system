Part I — Product foundation
1. Executive product definition
The Community Board System will be a secure digital platform for organizing community representation, managing thematic boards, receiving and resolving citizen complaints, monitoring public development projects, and documenting community decisions.

It will support a five-level organizational structure:
National Secretariat

Policy, governance standards and national monitoring

6 Zonal Coordinating Boards

Regional coordination and oversight

State / FCT Coordination

Operational support and jurisdictional reporting

774 LGA / Area Council Boards

Seven thematic boards and public-service interfaces

Ward Boards and Community Meeting Points

Citizen participation, cases and project observations


The uploaded YADEI document provides the national, zonal, LGA, ward and polling-unit organizational concept. I have added explicit state/FCT coordination as a proposed operational software layer; it is not a replacement for the original governance chart.

Official INEC delimitation data provides a baseline of 774 LGAs/area councils, 8,809 registration areas/wards and 176,846 polling units. These figures are also consistent with the source document's proposed footprint. 
Independent National Electoral Commission+1


Important architectural distinction: Polling units can be used as geographic references or community meeting locations, but the platform must not treat INEC polling units as CBS-owned institutions, electoral authorities or proof of a person's voting registration. Community membership will be based on verified service jurisdiction and residency, not political affiliation or voter preference.

Code	Thematic board	Primary oversight

These will be seeded as permanent reference definitions within the software.

The application will permit subcommittees under each board and additional thematic boards in future, but the seven principal boards cannot be deleted or silently renamed by an ordinary administrator.
3. Release boundaries
Version 1
Included: Identity and geographic onboarding; board membership and administration; citizen complaints and case management; projects and funding oversight; meetings, resolutions, notifications, search, reports and auditing.


Future release
Full early-warning command centre, direct emergency dispatch, extensive AI analytics, e-learning, trade marketplace and advanced interagency integrations.


Version 1 will still permit restricted safety complaints to be referred to an authorized organization, but it must not pretend to provide live emergency dispatch before appropriate agreements and operational capacity exist.
4. Product acceptance principles
A successful implementation must satisfy the following:
1. A resident can register and find the appropriate community jurisdiction without unnecessary data collection.
2. A board officer cannot operate outside their formally authorized jurisdiction.
3. Board appointment, tenure, conflicts and resignations are traceable.
4. Every complaint has an owner, progress history and review pathway.
5. Every monitored public project distinguishes documented facts from community observations.
6. Funding disclosures retain source evidence and verification status.
7. Every formal board resolution has an accountable meeting record, quorum result and voting record.
8. Confidential records cannot leak through public pages, search, notifications or exports.
9. No administrator can erase the historical audit trail through the ordinary application.
10. The platform works on low-bandwidth mobile connections and remains usable through assisted community access.

Geographic data requirements

The data model must maintain separate records for:

country → state/FCT → LGA/area council → ward → community/settlement

Polling units should be stored as a parallel electoral-geography reference linked to the applicable ward, not made mandatory ancestors of every settlement.

Each geographic record needs a stable internal UUID, official code where available, official name, alternative names, parent ID, effective date, status, source reference and optional verified boundary geometry.

The platform must handle spelling differences, renamed locations, ambiguous community names, missing mapping coordinates and future revisions without corrupting historical cases.
ID-06 — Residency verification
Supported methods should include manual verification by designated trained officers, authorized document review, verified community address records or approved institutional attestation.

Requirements:
- Store verification type and outcome separately from sensitive document contents.
- Make identity documentation optional unless specifically required for the selected action.
- Provide reasonable alternatives for residents without formal documentation.
- Restrict document visibility.
- Record verification expiry and reviewer identity.
- Provide an appeal route after rejection.
A verified resident may participate in the appropriate local community. Unverified residents should still be able to access public information and suitable complaint channels.
ID-08 — Citizen home dashboard
The citizen dashboard must display community name, applicable board directory, active reports, recent resolutions, public projects, meeting announcements and important notifications.

It should offer prominent actions for Report an Issue, View My Cases, Track Projects, and Community Meetings.

No confidential information about other residents should be displayed.

BD-04 to BD-06 — Nomination and appointment
The appointment process must include:
1. Candidate application or nomination.
2. Candidate notice and acceptance.
3. Identity and eligibility review.
4. Residency verification where required.
5. Professional or stakeholder qualification verification.
6. Conflict-of-interest disclosure.
7. Independent screening.
8. Recorded appointing-authority decision.
9. Digitally signed appointment record.
10. Activation of the member's specific permissions.
Separation-of-duties rule: The candidate, screening officer and final approver cannot be the same person for the same appointment.

A nomination must never automatically grant administrative access.
BD-08 — Member profile
Public-facing member information should include only approved information such as name, board position, service period, professional category and official contact channel.

Internal records may contain verified residency status, appointment history, required training, disciplinary restrictions and conflict disclosures, subject to strict access controls.

BD-12 — Tenure and removal
All appointment records must be effective-dated.

Revocation must terminate privileged sessions promptly. Removal requires a reason category, the competent authority, a documented decision, an appeal route and an audit trail.

The uploaded source recommends unpaid voluntary membership but does not fully determine all tenure, disciplinary and appointment procedures. These must remain configurable until the adopted governance charter supplies the controlling rules.

CS-07 — Triage and assignment
The triage officer interface must show the submitted report, jurisdiction, possible duplicates, sensitivity, board assignment options and issue category.

Actions:

Validate, Request Information, Assign, Refer, Mark Duplicate, Reject with Reason.

A duplicate case should be linked to a master issue while preserving the individual resident's submission and feedback rights.

Cases should not be rejected merely because they criticize public officials or board members.
CS-08 — Investigation workspace
Authorized handlers must be able to:
- Record factual observations and inspection notes.
- Request supplementary information.
- Attach verified evidence.
- Assign follow-up tasks.
- Record communication with agencies.
- Make a recommendation.
- Escalate unresolved matters.
- Add restricted internal notes separate from citizen-visible updates.
Every significant action must be recorded in a server-controlled event history.
CS-09 — Interagency referral
Referral records should contain receiving institution, authorized recipient, lawful purpose, limited data package, submission time, delivery confirmation, acknowledgement and follow-up status.

If an agency is not connected, the system should show an external referral route rather than falsely claiming successful electronic transmission.
CS-10 and CS-11 — Resolution and appeal
Case closure must distinguish:
- Action reported: The assigned officer says work was completed.
- Action verified: Supporting evidence has been reviewed.
- Citizen accepted: The complainant confirms satisfaction.
- Closed with disagreement: An authorized reviewer closes the matter while preserving the resident's objection.
- Reopened: New evidence or approved appeal triggers further work.
A citizen must be able to challenge closure, request correction and submit additional information through a defined review process.

Recommended case lifecycle

Draft → Submitted → Intake Review → Accepted → Assigned → In Progress → Outcome Proposed → Verification → Closed

Additional branches: Needs Information, Referred, Duplicate Linked, Rejected with Reasons, Disputed, Appealed, Reopened.

The distinction between a board decision and an actual government response must remain visible throughout.

The dual-key disclosure workflow
Funding / Project Notice Registered
Original document, issuing institution and amount
Implementing Authority

Acknowledges funding information and implementation responsibilities


Community Board

Receives monitoring notice and verifies project observations



Independent project-monitoring record
Evidence, milestones, inspection and public disclosure

The dual-key approach gives both implementers and community oversight bodies visibility, but the two parties are not given equal legal authority to control public funds.

If the implementing authority has not joined CBS, the application may register a verified publicly available notice and request an official response. It must not fabricate agency acknowledgements.
PR-06 — Milestone management
Milestones should include:

title, description, planned_date, actual_date, weight, acceptance_criteria, assigned_party, evidence_required, reported_percent, verified_percent, status.

The sum of active weighted milestones should equal 100%, with validation on approval of the project baseline.

Any change to approved cost, timeline, scope or milestone weights must produce a versioned amendment record.
PR-07 — Site monitoring
An authorized board monitor can record:
- Visit date and location.
- Inspector identity.
- Site access status.
- Observed work.
- Photographs and documents.
- Contractor or authority comments.
- Technical concerns.
- Recommendation.
- Follow-up actions.
A review of supplied photographs alone must not be falsely labeled as a physical site inspection.
PR-09 — Variance review
The application should flag potential differences such as:
- Project reported complete but no acceptance evidence.
- Milestone deadline exceeded.
- Released funding lacking related documentary evidence.
- Conflicting reported progress figures.
- Complaints about delivered work.
- Duplicate project entries.
- Missing source documents.
Flags should initially be rules-based and require human assessment. They should not automatically assert fraud, misconduct or criminal liability.
PR-10 — Completion verification
Completion should require a defined acceptance checklist, evidence, authorized technical confirmation where applicable, and independent board observation.

A project may be recorded as officially certified but disputed by the community, or community-observed complete but not officially certified. The distinction must not be erased.

MT-05 — Quorum and attendance
The system must calculate quorum using the board's approved charter and membership eligibility at the time of the meeting.

Suggested inputs:
- Eligible voting members.
- Required quorum rule.
- Confirmed attendance.
- Attendance verification method.
- Recorded recusals.
- Temporary restrictions.
- Meeting start and adjournment times.
Quorum calculations must be performed by the backend, never trusted to client-side input.

The uploaded document does not establish a uniform quorum threshold for all boards. This must be a versioned charter setting, not a hardcoded assumption.
MT-07 — Voting
Supported decision mechanisms may include:
- Voice or show-of-hands vote recorded by secretary.
- Recorded electronic vote for eligible members.
- Unanimous-consent decision.
- Deferred decision.
- Chairperson's procedural ruling where permitted.
The software should not offer anonymous formal ballots unless the approved rules support them and a suitable privacy-preserving voting design exists.

Voting must validate eligibility, quorum, conflict restrictions and the approved decision threshold.
MT-08 — Minutes
Minutes workflow:

Draft → Submitted for Review → Corrections Requested / Approved → Published or Restricted → Archived

Approved minutes should be locked against silent modification. Corrections require an amendment referencing the earlier version.

Meeting recordings, when permitted, should have recording notices, access restrictions and defined retention schedules.

15.7 Key database constraints
- A user may have multiple historical residencies but only one designated current primary service community at a time.
- A board type must be unique for an active jurisdiction, unless a charter explicitly authorizes multiple instances.
- An appointment cannot grant permissions outside its effective dates.
- A conflicted member cannot vote on a restricted matter.
- A project milestone belongs to an immutable project baseline version.
- Financial records must support signed corrections and reversals rather than silent overwrites.
- Meeting votes must be unique per member and motion, except where a formal revote is recorded as a distinct round.
- Case events and audit events must preserve historical order and provenance.

18.2 API conventions
- Use validated schemas and strict enum values.
- Use cursor-based pagination for large lists.
- Support idempotency keys for sensitive submissions.
- Use optimistic concurrency controls for versioned records.
- Return consistent error codes and trace identifiers.
- Apply server-side field filtering to prevent data leakage.
- Rate-limit public submission and authentication endpoints.
- Record authorization denials and high-risk actions.
- Use secure, short-lived object-storage upload URLs.
- Scan files before permitted retrieval or publication.
- Disallow arbitrary mass assignment in request bodies.

Part VI — Security, governance, compliance and quality assurance
21. Security architecture
The platform will contain potentially sensitive personal, financial and governance information. Security should therefore be implemented as a mandatory system-wide requirement.
21.1 Data classifications
Classification	Examples	Access rule
Public	Published board directory, approved resolutions, public project disclosures	Available without login
Internal	Board tasks, unpublished minutes, internal work queues	Authorized members only
Confidential	Complaints, resident verification documents, protected correspondence	Assigned authorized personnel
Highly restricted	Safety incidents, protected witness information, sensitive allegations	Explicitly approved case handlers
Security administration	Credentials, security logs, encryption operations	Segregated technical security roles

Critical principle: A system administrator should not automatically have authority to read confidential case content simply because they manage the application's infrastructure.

Sensitive records should have field-level or application-level encryption where appropriate, with carefully managed keys and privileged access procedures.
21.2 Security controls
Implement:
- Strong authentication and MFA for privileged users.
- Short-lived sessions and refresh-token rotation.
- Privileged session revocation when appointments expire.
- Jurisdiction-aware authorization on every protected request.
- File scanning, MIME validation and size restrictions.
- Protection against SQL injection, XSS, CSRF, SSRF and insecure direct-object references.
- Secure password and recovery policies where local authentication exists.
- Sensitive data redaction in logs and telemetry.
- Tamper-evident, append-only audit logs.
- Backups with protected storage and tested recovery.
- Security alerts for unusual account activity and bulk data access.
- Periodic independent penetration testing.
- Formal breach response and incident escalation procedures.
21.3 Nigerian legal and data-protection safeguards
The legal implementation plan must account for the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and relevant Nigeria Data Protection Commission guidance, including lawful processing, privacy notices, data protection impact assessments, organizational accountability and cross-border transfer requirements. 
National Drug Policy Committee


The governance framework must also recognize local-government constitutional arrangements and the implications of the July 11, 2024 Supreme Court judgment on local-government financial autonomy. 
Federal Ministry of Info


Before launch, legal counsel should approve the board charter, institutional agreements, privacy documentation, authorized case-handling model, disclosure rules and retention schedule.
21.4 Special safeguards for the Security Board
The Board of Security and Intelligence Gathering remains one of the seven principal boards, but it requires tighter protections than ordinary service boards.

For Version 1:
- Permit confidential safety reports and authorized referrals.
- Do not expose a public map of identifiable security complaints.
- Do not enable community-wide tracking of people's movements.
- Do not produce automated criminal suspicion scores.
- Do not enable warrantless searches, arrests or detention orders.
- Do not disclose reporter identity to ordinary board members.
- Do not allow accusations to be publicly published without appropriate review.
- Provide emergency-service guidance without claiming the platform is a 24/7 dispatch service.
All law-enforcement and emergency-response integrations require institutional approval, authenticated endpoints and tested operating procedures.

These are required demonstration capabilities, not claims that the application has already been built.
23. Deployment and infrastructure
I recommend using separate development, staging and production environments.

A typical pilot infrastructure should contain:
- Secure CDN and web application firewall.
- Next.js web hosting.
- Containerized NestJS API.
- Background worker deployment.
- Managed PostgreSQL with backups.
- Private document storage.
- Managed Redis.
- Identity provider.
- Central secrets and key-management service.
- Monitoring, tracing and audit infrastructure.
Production data should never be copied into development without approved de-identification.
23.1 CI/CD requirements
Every pull request should pass:
1. Code formatting and linting.
2. Type checks.
3. Unit tests.
4. API contract tests.
5. Dependency and secret scans.
6. Static security analysis.
7. Database migration checks.
8. End-to-end tests for critical workflows.
9. Authorized review before production deployment.
Database migrations must have rollback or forward-recovery procedures.
23.2 Operational monitoring
Operational dashboards should monitor API latency, error rates, authentication failures, background queue delays, notification delivery, database health, storage utilization, audit-log integrity and backup completion.

Application operators should have an incident-response runbook, documented on-call responsibilities and tested restoration procedures before public launch.
24. Suggested software delivery stages

Stage A — Design and governance approval

Finalize design tokens, all screen wireframes, user journeys, board charter rules, authorization matrix, privacy requirements and pilot jurisdictions.




Stage B — Core platform construction

Build identity, geography, role management, database schema, organization registry, boards, nominations and appointment workflows.




Stage C — Operational workflows

Build reporting, case triage, referrals, project registration, funding evidence, inspections, meetings and resolutions.




Stage D — Assurance and pilot

Complete security tests, accessibility review, legal sign-off, performance testing, onboarding documentation, training and controlled production pilot.




Stage E — Evaluation and scale-up

Analyze verified user adoption, service response, security performance, project-disclosure quality, governance compliance and pilot feedback before wider deployment.



Do not launch the entire national structure simultaneously. Begin with willing pilot communities and boards whose members, public-service counterparts and administrative staff can be trained and supported.

27. Recommended build sequence for Codex
For actual construction, the work should be divided into controlled engineering milestones.
1. Repository foundation: Monorepo, TypeScript, design system, testing tools, CI/CD and secure configuration.
2. Identity and geography: Authentication, profiles, residency verification, hierarchical jurisdiction registry and policies.
3. Board administration: Seven boards, nominations, appointments, committees, conflicts and tenure management.
4. Citizen cases: Reporting interface, triage, evidence, case timeline, referrals, outcome verification and appeals.
5. Project oversight: Project registry, source documents, financial events, milestones, inspections and transparency portal.
6. Meetings and resolutions: Scheduling, attendance, quorum, voting, minutes, resolutions and follow-up actions.
7. Cross-cutting assurance: Notifications, audit logging, reporting, privacy operations and security testing.
8. Pilot readiness: Deployment, sample data, staff training, operational documentation and end-to-end acceptance tests.
Each milestone should include migrations, API documentation, functional interfaces, tests and operational documentation. Avoid producing disconnected mockups or frontend components that are not connected to properly authorized backend workflows.
28. Strategic conclusion
The Community Board System should be built as an institutional-grade grassroots governance application, with the citizen as the primary beneficiary and the community board as the accountable coordination mechanism.

The first version must do five things exceptionally well: know which community a resident belongs to, establish legitimate community board membership, process complaints fairly, make development spending and progress traceable, and record transparent board decisions.

These functions create the foundation for the later integration of security early-warning systems, socioeconomic programmes, development analytics and broader national coordination.

The next logical engineering deliverable is a Codex-ready implementation package, organized into executable build instructions, complete database migrations, API schemas, frontend screen definitions, role policies and automated test requirements. It should be generated directly from this specification, preserving its safeguards and avoiding unnecessary changes to the agreed five-module priority.

That will allow software development to proceed systematically rather than relying on a single oversized prompt that could omit important business rules or security controls.
